Laisamis, Marsabit County, Kenya · Women led since 2008

We stand between girls in Laisamis and the cut, the beads, and the early marriage.

Feiyah Action Network is run by Samburu and Rendille women in the community where the work happens, not from an office in Nairobi. Since 2008 we have challenged FGM/C, child marriage, and child beading through advocacy, community dialogue, and direct intervention.

Our mission, in our own words.

To change the mentality of girls and their communities on FGM/C, child marriage, and child beading through empowerment, protection, and advocacy, paving the way for a future where girls make informed decisions about their own bodies.

Our vision is a society where girls and boys are recognised as equal individuals, with the right to live self determined lives, free from violence and harmful cultural practices.

Rose Orguba founded FAN in 2008 and registered it in 2010. She still leads it today, alongside staff and volunteers who come from the communities they serve.

Three practices end a girl's childhood here.

FGM/C

Female genital mutilation and cutting remains common in pastoralist communities in Marsabit, despite being illegal in Kenya since 2011. It is often the moment a girl stops being treated as a child.

Child marriage

Girls are married young, frequently soon after being cut. Marriage and distance to school are two of the main reasons girls in Laisamis drop out during their teenage years.

Child beading

In beading, a young man places beads on a girl to claim her for a relationship the community never treats as marriage. The girl carries the consequences, including pregnancies that bring her no standing and no support.

Three programs, one community.

Outside campaigns have come and gone in Laisamis for years. What holds is change the community decides on itself, through participatory dialogue that brings in elders, youth, men, and boys rather than speaking only to women.

Girls' education

Distance, cost, early marriage, and FGM all interrupt a girl's schooling. FAN runs classroom support sessions with local schools, distributes sanitary supplies so girls do not miss days each month, and backs a girls' football team that keeps them tied to school life beyond lessons.

Community land rights

Community land in Marsabit is being registered and managed in rooms women are rarely invited into, though they depend on that land daily for grazing, water, and food. FAN runs women's circles on how land governance works and how to take part in a Community Land Management Committee.

Komesha FGM Sasa

Our gender justice and protection campaign, run with ActionAid and UNFPA, brings whole villages together at once, including young men, to confront FGM and gender based violence in public rather than household by household.
